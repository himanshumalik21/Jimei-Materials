Process Science

High-Temperature Eutectic Bonding Process

Direct Bonded Copper (DBC) substrates are manufactured using a high-temperature gas-metal eutectic reaction. High-purity oxygen-free copper (OFC) foil and ceramic plates (Al₂O₃ or AlN) are heated in a controlled nitrogen/oxygen atmosphere furnace to precisely 1065°C – 1083°C.

At this temperature, a liquid Cu-O eutectic melt phase forms at the interface, wetting both the copper foil and ceramic substrate simultaneously. Upon cooling, the copper forms an atomic chemical bond with the ceramic with shear strength exceeding >20 MPa, combining the electrical conductivity of bulk copper with the electrical isolation and mechanical rigidity of ceramic.

Following bonding, circuit layouts are patterned using chemical etching or precision laser ablation, followed by surface plating (electroless nickel/gold or solderable coatings) and 100% optical/electrical verification.

High-Power Deployment

Key Applications for DBC Substrates

  • EV Traction & On-Board Chargers (OBC): High current density busbars and power switching stages in hybrid and electric vehicle powertrains.
  • IGBT Power Modules: Standard half-bridge and six-pack industrial motor drive modules operating at 600V to 1700V.
  • Solar & Wind Inverters: Utility-scale renewable power converters requiring long lifespan and high thermal cycling resilience.
  • Solid-State Relays & Welder Power Supplies: Robust, mechanically rigid power carrier boards with high dielectric isolation.
